This project presents interactive visualizations of vegetation products from MODIS, Landsat 9, and Sentinel-2 satellite datasets. Using NDVI and False Color Composite (FCC) imagery, it enables users to explore vegetation distribution, monitor land cover, and compare satellite observations across different spatial resolutions.

Applications
Drought & crop monitoring

The MODIS NDVI (2024) map represents the vegetation condition across the Goa talukas of Mormugao, Tiswadi, and Bardez, which form the Region of Interest (ROI). Most of the ROI is covered by moderate to healthy vegetation (green shades), indicating good vegetation health. Dark green areas represent dense vegetation and forest cover, while yellow and orange areas indicate sparse vegetation, agricultural land, or built-up regions with lower vegetation density. Blue areas correspond to water bodies and regions with very low vegetation. Overall, the map provides a clear overview of the spatial distribution of vegetation across the selected Goa talukas and demonstrates the usefulness of MODIS NDVI for regional vegetation monitoring.

The Landsat 9 False Color Composite (B5–B4–B3) shows the land cover of the Goa talukas of Mormugao, Tiswadi, and Bardez. In this image, healthy vegetation appears in bright red, indicating that a large part of the study area is covered with vegetation. Light red shades represent moderate vegetation and croplands. Grey and brown areas indicate built-up land and bare soil, while dark blue and black areas represent water bodies such as rivers, estuaries, and the Arabian Sea. Overall, the image provides a clear view of vegetation and other land cover features across the selected talukas of Goa.

So how does it help us?

This project demonstrates the use of satellite remote sensing data to visualize vegetation through three different datasets: MODIS NDVI, Landsat 9 False Color Composite (FCC), and Sentinel-2 False Color Composite (FCC). Each dataset provides a unique perspective on vegetation based on its spatial and temporal characteristics, allowing users to explore and compare vegetation patterns effectively. By integrating these visualizations into an interactive web application, the project makes Earth observation data more accessible and easier to understand. It highlights the importance of satellite imagery in monitoring vegetation, studying land cover, and supporting environmental analysis. Overall, the project showcases how modern geospatial technologies can transform complex satellite data into meaningful visual insights for education, research, and environmental monitoring.
