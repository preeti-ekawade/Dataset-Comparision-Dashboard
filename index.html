The MODIS NDVI (2024) map represents the vegetation condition across the Goa talukas of Mormugao, Tiswadi, and Bardez, which form the Region of Interest (ROI). Most of the ROI is covered by moderate to healthy vegetation (green shades), indicating good vegetation health. Dark green areas represent dense vegetation and forest cover, while yellow and orange areas indicate sparse vegetation, agricultural land, or built-up regions with lower vegetation density. Blue areas correspond to water bodies and regions with very low vegetation. Overall, the map provides a clear overview of the spatial distribution of vegetation across the selected Goa talukas and demonstrates the usefulness of MODIS NDVI for regional vegetation monitoring.

So how does it help us?

Inference

This project demonstrates the use of satellite remote sensing data to visualize vegetation through three different datasets: MODIS NDVI, Landsat 9 False Color Composite (FCC), and Sentinel-2 False Color Composite (FCC). Each dataset provides a unique perspective on vegetation based on its spatial and temporal characteristics, allowing users to explore and compare vegetation patterns effectively.

By integrating these visualizations into an interactive web application, the project makes Earth observation data more accessible and easier to understand. It highlights the importance of satellite imagery in monitoring vegetation, studying land cover, and supporting environmental analysis. Overall, the project showcases how modern geospatial technologies can transform complex satellite data into meaningful visual insights for education, research, and environmental monitoring.
